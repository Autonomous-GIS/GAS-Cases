Case 3

County-level choropleth map of 2021 population for the contiguous United States

In the third use case, we asked the system to create a county-level choropleth map of 2021 population for the contiguous United States using Lambert Conformal Conic projection, by classifying the data into five quantile classes. The coordinator translated the request into a six-step workflow that retrieved county boundaries, filtered them to the contiguous United States, retrieved a county-level population table, joined the tabular values to county polygons, reprojected the result, and generated the final choropleth map. The workflow shows that the coordinator must preserve downstream suitability, not only assign tasks to agents. The boundary layer had to retain stable join identifiers, the tabular population data had to be transformed into a join-ready form, and the resulting spatial dataset had to be projected into a map-appropriate coordinate reference system before visualization. In this case, the DataRetrieverAgent handled boundary and tabular data collection, the VectorAnalysisAgent performed spatial filtering and attribute joining, the MapProjectionAgent carried out reprojection, and the MappingAgent produced the final map. The completed workflow took 199.11 seconds, used four specialized agents, and finished without replanning. This case highlights geospatial interoperability and planning in a workflow that combines spatial and non-spatial data. The success of the final map depended on maintaining compatibility across identifiers, schema, spatial extent, and projection choices throughout the workflow. By making these transformations explicit service steps rather than hidden operations, the system kept the full process traceable and reproducible.
